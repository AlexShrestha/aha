Severity, so you know where to start.

Every product gets one severity and three scores: Asset Coverage, Variant Integrity and SEO Accessibility. Sort by severity and work down.

CriticalSev 1
  • A color you sell has no photo
  • Wrong photo on a variant
  • Different colors share one gallery
  • No usable main image
HighSev 2
  • Missing back, side or detail shot
  • No on-model shot for fit-driven products
  • Category shots missing (sole, inside, scale)
MediumSev 3
  • Backgrounds or lighting drift within a gallery
  • Mixed crops and aspect ratios
  • Near-duplicates, weak gallery order
  • Text burned into images
LowSev 4
  • Missing or weak alt text
  • Filenames like IMG_2041.jpg
  • One slightly redundant shot

SEO problems are always reported but never push a product above Low. Size-only variants that look identical aren't flagged.

Questions merchants ask first.

Will it change anything in my store?

No. Asset Audit requests read access to products only. It never edits products, images, themes or settings. You make the fixes, in Shopify, the way you normally would.

What's the difference between the free scan and the app?

The free scan reads your public catalog and checks structure: how many photos each product has, color variants with no photo, mixed crops, low resolution, generic filenames and a sample of alt text. The app adds AI vision on the photos themselves: background and lighting drift, which angles are present, and which shots each category is missing.

Does the free scan send my data anywhere?

It runs in your browser against the same public product feed any visitor to your store can load. Results aren't sent to us or stored. We count that a scan happened, not what it found.

My store is password-protected. Can I still use it?

The free scan can't read a locked storefront, but the app can. It audits from inside your admin, so it works before launch too.

Do my variants need their own photos?

Only when they look different. A color or finish with no photo is critical: the shopper picks it and sees something else. Sizes that look identical can share images, and the audit won't flag them.
